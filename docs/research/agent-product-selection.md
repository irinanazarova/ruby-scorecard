# What makes an agent pick your product: selection after discoverability

Research report for the ruby-scorecard project, August 2026. The project's existing framing (training vs retrieval, in `app/views/pages/learn.html.erb`) covers whether a model *can know* you. This report covers the next gate: given that the model or agent can find you, what determines whether it *chooses* you. The short version, supported below: selection is a two-layer system that mirrors the project's existing two-system framing. A **parametric prior** set at training time (how often the tool appears next to the problem it solves, in permissively collected text) decides the default, and **whatever is in the context window at answer time** is the only force measured to reliably override that default. Content-styling tricks on your website sit between those two layers and have the weakest evidence of all.

---

## 1. Academic and industry research on how generative engines choose

### 1.1 The GEO paper (KDD 2024): content changes can move citation visibility, with caveats

The founding academic work is "GEO: Generative Engine Optimization" (Aggarwal et al., KDD 2024), which tested nine content-modification strategies over 10,000 queries against search-grounded generative engines ([arXiv:2311.09735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735), [ACM version](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/3637528.3671900)). Measured effects, by position-adjusted word count of the source in the answer:

- **Quotation addition: +41%.** Statistics addition: +31%. Citing sources: +30%. Fluency optimization: +27% ([paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09735v3)).
- **Keyword stuffing performed at or below baseline.** The classic SEO tactic transfers negatively.
- **GEO helped low-ranked sites most**: cite-sources gave a 115% visibility increase for sites ranked fifth in the SERP while the top-ranked site's visibility fell 30% ([paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09735v3)). Effects were domain-dependent (statistics helped most in law/government and opinion domains).

Scope caveat that matters for the framing: GEO measures **citation share in retrieval-grounded answers**. It says nothing about which product the model recommends from parametric memory when no search happens.

### 1.2 C-SEO Bench (NeurIPS 2025): the main replication says most of those tactics don't work

**A peer-reviewed replication found most conversational-SEO methods largely ineffective.** "C-SEO Bench: Does Conversational SEO Work?" (Puerto, Gubri et al., NeurIPS 2025 Datasets & Benchmarks) evaluated nine C-SEO methods across six domains and two tasks and reports that "most current C-SEO methods are largely ineffective, contrary to reported results in the literature," while a traditional retrieval baseline, moving your document to position 1 in the retrieved context, was roughly 7.6x more effective than the best content-editing method ([arXiv:2506.11097](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11097), [code](https://github.com/parameterlab/c-seo-bench), [NeurIPS page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/121465)).

This is the single most decision-relevant academic result in the whole space: **being retrieved and placed early in context beats every known way of styling your prose by a wide margin.** Follow-on academic work continues on both sides ([IF-GEO, 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.13938); ["Generative Engine Optimization at Scale"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.20065)), so treat any specific GEO tactic percentage as contested.

### 1.3 Tool-selection bias research: descriptions and pre-training exposure drive agent choices

- **BiasBusters (2025): LLMs systematically prefer some functionally equivalent tools over others.** Across seven models, the strongest driver of tool selection was semantic similarity between the user's query and the tool's *description* (descriptions mattered far more than names); positional bias in the tool list persisted in some models; and, critically for the training-time story, saturating continued pre-training with one provider's metadata increased that provider's selection share roughly **20x** ([arXiv:2510.00307](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.00307)).
- **LLMs' library preferences are heavily concentrated and popularity-driven.** "A Study of LLMs' Preferences for Libraries and Programming Languages" (2025) found eight models used only 32 to 39 distinct Python libraries across hundreds of varied tasks (out of 7,000+ viable packages), inserted NumPy into 45% of tasks that didn't need it, chose Python for 58% of tasks where it was suboptimal, and never once picked Rust. The authors attribute this to training-data composition and note newer, technically stronger alternatives (Polars, FastAPI) barely appear ([arXiv:2503.17181](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.17181v3)).
- **Related agent-behavior findings:** agents drift to over-privileged tools when equivalents exist ([arXiv:2606.20023](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.20023v1)), and library-name hallucination is common enough to have spawned an attack class ("slopsquatting"): ~20% of packages recommended across 576,000 generated samples didn't exist, and 43% of hallucinated names repeated across all 10 re-queries, meaning the model's package-name prior is stable even when wrong ([USENIX ;login:](https://www.usenix.org/publications/loginonline/we-have-package-you-comprehensive-analysis-package-hallucinations-code), [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2025/04/14/package-hallucination-slopsquatting-malicious-code/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slopsquatting)).

### 1.4 Brand-recommendation measurement studies (industry, mixed rigor)

- **Seer Interactive (solid methodology for its class): Google page-1 rankings correlate ~0.65 with LLM brand mentions; backlinks correlate weakly or not at all.** 300K+ keywords in finance/SaaS, 10,000 queries run through GPT-4o ([Seer study](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/what-drives-brand-mentions-in-ai-answers)). Seer also published an 800K-response study on review profiles ([link](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/study-of-800k-ai-responses-how-reviews-shape-brand-presence-in-ai-search)) and a recency study ([link](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/study-ai-brand-visibility-and-content-recency)).
- **Ahrefs (75K brands): off-site brand mentions predict AI visibility ~3x better than backlinks.** YouTube mentions correlate 0.737, branded web mentions 0.664, branded anchors 0.527, branded search volume 0.392, backlinks only 0.218 ([Ahrefs blog](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-brand-visibility-correlations), [third-party summary](https://www.citeflow.io/blog/brand-mentions-vs-backlinks)). Correlational only: models mention brands people already talk about, which is also just what popularity looks like.
- **Profound (6.8M citations, 1.6M responses): 82 to 85% of AI citations are third-party sources, and Reddit is the most-cited source across engines.** Gemini leans on brand-owned sites (52% of citations), ChatGPT on third-party consensus ([summary](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/ai-share-of-voice-tracking-brand-citations-framework-2026)). Profound sells GEO tooling; treat magnitudes as directional.
- **Vendor-claim to flag, currently unverifiable:** several GEO-vendor pages claim external signals explain only ~5.8% of variance in LLM recommendation scores, with "80-85% inside the model" ([The Digital Bloom](https://thedigitalbloom.com/learn/llm-ranking-factors-2026/)). This could not be traced to a primary dataset; the same site's flagship report does not contain the figure ([their report](https://thedigitalbloom.com/learn/2025-ai-citation-llm-visibility-report/)). Directionally it matches the academic picture (prior dominates), but do not cite the number.
- **Evertune** ($19M raised, Trade Desk founders) polls 11 models with thousands of prompt runs to compute an "AI Brand Score" (visibility frequency x position weighting), which is the emerging industry definition of "share of model voice" ([Evertune](https://www.evertune.ai/products/ai-brand-index)). Methodology point worth stealing for the scorecard: single prompts are noise; the industry standard is now repeated prompt panels over time, consistent with the project's own recall-probe variance finding.

## 2. Training-time selection factors (slow, corpus-wide)

**Corpus prevalence sets a prior, and the prior compounds.** The library-preference study above is the cleanest evidence: models converge on the most-trained stack and alternatives get "statistically buried." The most vivid demonstration is frontend: give any of Claude, GPT, Gemini, Grok, DeepSeek, or Qwen a bare "build me a todo app" and all of them produce Next.js/Vite + TypeScript + Tailwind + shadcn/ui ([six-model experiment](https://saschb2b.com/blog/llm-default-react-stack), [Schwarzmüller essay](https://maximilian-schwarzmueller.com/articles/the-problem-with-the-default-ai-stack/)). The mechanism analysis identifies three causes worth internalizing:

1. **Task-to-tool association in training data.** The stack that appears most often next to "build an app" becomes the completion for "build an app." This is prevalence *in context of the problem*, which is stronger than raw prevalence. (This is also why AnyCable's real competitor in the corpus is every Stack Overflow answer that says "use Action Cable" under a websockets question.)
2. **Model-ergonomic design.** Tailwind wins partly because utility classes are atomic, predictable tokens; shadcn wins because the code is copied into the repo where the model can read and edit it, with no opaque API to memorize. Tools whose usage is visible plain code in the user's project are structurally easier for models to work with, and get picked for it.
3. **A feedback loop.** Default output gets committed to GitHub, future models train on it, the default tightens. First-mover advantage in the corpus is durable.

**Sentiment and community discussion enter through both training and retrieval, and Reddit is the dominant single surface.** Reddit is the most-cited domain across AI engines (~40% citation frequency in Profound's data, cited above), OpenAI and Google both have Reddit licensing deals, and manipulation research shows the channel's power directly: a Cornell Tech study found snippets as short as 13 words in a single poisoned Reddit comment influenced deep-research agent outputs across a whole cluster of related queries ([404 Media](https://www.404media.co/it-is-trivially-easy-to-use-reddit-to-manipulate-ai-search-research-suggests/), [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/06/23/reddit-ai-search-poisoning-research/)). Firms now openly sell Reddit-seeding as "AEO," and subreddit mods are fighting coordinated promotional content ([Slashdot summary](https://tech.slashdot.org/story/26/06/04/1828244/companies-are-using-reddit-to-manipulate-chatgpt-and-google-ai-search)). Evidence that *negative* sentiment suppresses recommendation is thinner; the well-supported claim is that volume and consensus of third-party mentions matter, with sentiment as a plausible modifier.

**Staleness is measured and severe: models emit deprecated APIs at 25 to 38% rates.** An ICSE 2025 study measured deprecated-API usage across eight Python libraries at 25-38% of completions ([ICSE 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1109/ICSE55347.2025.00245), [PDF](https://yebof.github.io/assets/pdf/wang2025icse.pdf)), and follow-up work shows RAG only partially fixes it because models suffer "context-memory conflict," trusting stale parametric knowledge over the fresh docs in context ([arXiv:2604.09515](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09515)). Retrieval behavior amplifies the recency pressure from the other side: ~65% of AI-bot content hits target pages published within the past year, 79% within two years ([Seer recency study](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/study-ai-brand-visibility-and-content-recency), [aggregate](https://thedigitalbloom.com/learn/2025-ai-citation-llm-visibility-report/)). Implication: a tool whose corpus footprint is dominated by an old major version gets recommended in its old idiom, and the fix has to be delivered in context (see 3.4).

## 3. Inference-time selection factors (fast, per-question)

The unifying finding, from C-SEO Bench's 7.6x result through the BiasBusters description finding: **whatever occupies the context window at answer time wins.** The channels below differ mainly in who controls the injection.

### 3.1 llms.txt: widely adopted, near-zero measured consumption

The /learn page's position holds up as of August 2026. ~10% adoption across 300K domains, but server-log monitoring across 500M+ AI-bot visits in 90 days found only 408 requests for llms.txt files; GPTBot, ClaudeBot, and PerplexityBot fetch HTML directly ([adoption evidence review](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/llms-txt-in-practice-adoption-evidence-2026)). Google explicitly declined to endorse it ([Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-does-not-endorse-llms-txt-40789.html)). One genuinely new development: Google added an llms.txt audit to Lighthouse in May 2026 under "agentic browsing audits" ([same review](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/llms-txt-in-practice-adoption-evidence-2026)), the first signal any major vendor might route on it. Verdict stays "informational, never a win," worth one re-check in six months.

### 3.2 MCP servers: a real distribution channel with power-law outcomes

Publishing an MCP server is now standard devtools go-to-market ([Anthropic MCP announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/model-context-protocol)); a 2026 study of 177,000 MCP tools finds usage concentrates on a small head of tools, with documentation quality and functionality alignment driving adoption ([arXiv:2603.23802](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.23802)). Combined with BiasBusters: once a server is installed, the *tool description text* is the main lever for whether the agent invokes it, and it should be written to semantically match the user phrasings you want to capture ("realtime updates," "broadcast to browsers," "resize images"), never just to name your API.

### 3.3 Agent instruction files and skills: the newest and fastest-moving channel

- **AGENTS.md is now a Linux Foundation standard read by 20+ tools, present in 60,000+ repos** ([guide](https://codersera.com/blog/agents-md-complete-guide-2026/), [comparison](https://codersera.com/blog/agents-md-vs-claude-md-vs-cursor-rules-comparison-2026/)). Whatever the file names as the project's cable/auth/queue choice is, for practical purposes, the agent's decision. Starter templates that ship an AGENTS.md naming your tool make you the default for every project generated from them.
- **Agent Skills became an open standard in December 2025 at agentskills.io, adopted by Microsoft, OpenAI, Cursor, GitHub, and ~40 products.** Anthropic's launch partner directory (Atlassian, Figma, Stripe, Notion, Sentry, Zapier...) shows vendors shipping first-party skills as distribution ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/anthropic-launches-enterprise-agent-skills-and-opens-the-standard), [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-publishes-agent-skills-as-an-open-standard-for-ai-platforms/), [The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/agent-skills-anthropics-next-bid-to-define-ai-standards/)). A vendor skill is the strongest possible context injection: your install steps, your idioms, and your product name loaded into the agent at exactly the moment the matching task appears. No published measurement yet quantifies skill-driven selection lift; the channel is too new. (This repo's own `anycable-hotwire-setup` skill is precisely this play.)
- **Scaffolding defaults are absolute.** v0 is stack-locked to React/Next/Tailwind/shadcn ([experiment writeup](https://saschb2b.com/blog/llm-default-react-stack)); `rails new` defaults (Solid Cable, Solid Queue) play the same role in Ruby. Being in the generator output of a popular scaffold outranks every other channel because it removes the choice entirely.

### 3.4 Search-grounded answers: classic ranking is the entry ticket

For agents that search mid-answer (ChatGPT search, Claude with web search, Perplexity), Seer's ~0.65 correlation with Google page-1 rank plus C-SEO's position-1 result imply the boring conclusion: **rank in the retrieval layer, then make the fetched page answer the question in its first screenful.** Recency bias (Section 2) means refreshed pages with visible dates get fetched more; Vercel's published playbook institutionalizes 30/90/180-day content refresh ([case study](https://aiseotracker.com/case-study/vercel)).

## 4. Empirical measurements of tool selection specifically

- **Vercel: ChatGPT referrals went from under 1% of signups (Oct 2024) to 10% (Apr 2025), their fastest-growing acquisition channel** ([case study](https://aiseotracker.com/case-study/vercel)). The measured fact is the funnel number; the attributed causes (definitive docs, community citations, refresh cadence) are Vercel's own interpretation.
- **Auth: practitioner consensus, with no controlled study found, is that Cursor and Claude Code default to Auth.js because it dominates open-source examples**, and one comparison states the point cleanly: "an AI tool's default auth suggestion is not a recommendation but whatever appeared most in its training data" ([Vibe Coder comparison](https://blog.vibecoder.me/clerk-vs-authjs-vs-supabase-auth)).
- **Frontend: the six-model todo-app experiment (Section 2) is the cleanest reproducible task-level measurement published.**
- **Nobody has published a Rails-specific tool-selection benchmark** (websockets: Action Cable vs AnyCable vs Solid Cable; jobs: Sidekiq vs Solid Queue vs GoodJob). Direct search found nothing. The language-level question is already answered in-house by [chad/whichlang](https://github.com/chad/whichlang): Ruby 0 of 1,265 samples across 11 models, even on Rails-home-turf tasks (B2B SaaS, issue tracker, team inbox), so the contested ground is tool choice inside an ecosystem already selected by the repo or the human. This is an open lane the scorecard project is unusually well positioned to fill, with the repeated-prompt-panel methodology from Section 1.4, and it would likely be widely cited given how much of this market runs on vendor content.
- **Evidence-quality summary:** peer-reviewed and strong: GEO, C-SEO Bench, BiasBusters, library-preference and deprecated-API studies. Large-N but correlational and vendor-run: Ahrefs, Profound, Seer. Single-company anecdote: Vercel. Marketing content wearing a lab coat: most "LLM ranking factors 2026" pages (The Digital Bloom, GetAISO, MaxAEO, UltraScout), useful for hypotheses only.

## 5. Ranked levers for Evil Martians (AnyCable, imgproxy, PostCSS, the Ruby ecosystem)

Ordered by evidence strength x expected effect x feasibility. Inference-time levers first because they act on the next question asked, which matches the project's existing training/retrieval split.

### Inference-time (fast)

1. **Ship and promote agent skills and AGENTS.md snippets for each product.** Strongest theoretical channel (direct context injection at task time, per C-SEO Bench's in-context dominance), already standardized across vendors (agentskills.io), zero measured downside. AnyCable has a working in-house prototype in this repo (`anycable-hotwire-setup`); productize it, submit to marketplaces and the partner directory, and publish copy-pasteable AGENTS.md blocks in every product's docs ("add this to your AGENTS.md"). Same for imgproxy (an "image processing" skill) and PostCSS.
2. **Get into scaffolds and templates.** The v0/rails-new evidence says defaults end the conversation. Concrete moves: AnyCable one-command install in popular Rails starter kits (Jumpstart, Bullet Train, Suspenders), an official `rails new`-adjacent generator, imgproxy in deploy templates (Kamal accessories, Fly/Render one-clicks, Coolify). Each template adoption converts every downstream generated project.
3. **Write MCP/tool descriptions and README openers to semantically match user problem phrasings.** BiasBusters: description semantics are the dominant selection driver. The AnyCable MCP server or skill description should contain "realtime," "WebSockets at scale," "Turbo Streams," "presence," "live updates in Rails," because those are the query strings agents match against.
4. **Refresh cadence plus visible dates on key docs pages.** Recency bias is one of the best-replicated retrieval findings (65% of bot hits target pages under a year old). Cheap, and it also counteracts the stale-version problem for AnyCable's pre-v1 corpus footprint.
5. **First-screen answers with statistics and quotable numbers on comparison-shaped pages.** GEO's +31% statistics / +41% quotation findings, discounted by C-SEO Bench's failed replication: worth doing on pages that get retrieved into answers ("AnyCable vs Action Cable performance," imgproxy benchmark pages), with modest expectations. This dovetails with the existing FineWeb-Edu lead-rewrite work; the same rewrite serves both filters.

### Training-time (slow, compounding)

6. **Maximize task-adjacent presence in permissively licensed code: runnable examples, cookbooks, and the canonical copy-pasteable snippet.** This is where the project's own measured results (copies predict recall; code channel has no prose filter) meet the external literature (pre-training exposure shifts tool selection ~20x in BiasBusters; Supabase/Resend recall via copied snippets). The MIT-licensed AnyCable cookbook repo and tutorial twins already planned are exactly the right instrument. The goal formulated precisely: **make AnyCable the code that appears next to the problem it solves**, in many independently copied repos.
7. **Earn third-party mentions where corpora and engines actually look: Reddit, YouTube, Stack Overflow answers.** Ahrefs' 0.737 YouTube and 0.664 web-mention correlations and Profound's Reddit dominance agree here, and it plays to Evil Martians' existing conference/talk strength (record everything, get it on YouTube with transcripts). Answer the "websockets in Rails at scale" questions on Reddit/SO honestly under real names; leave the astroturfing to companies that want to be a 404 Media story.
8. **Design for model ergonomics in the products themselves.** The shadcn lesson: tools whose usage lives as plain, readable, editable code in the user's repo are structurally favored by LLMs over tools hidden behind opaque configuration. Generators that write visible, idiomatic Ruby (rather than magic), and config in formats models predict well, make every future model better at recommending and using the product.
9. **Publish the missing benchmark: "which tools do models pick for Rails tasks."** Fills a measured gap (Section 4), earns exactly the third-party citations lever 7 wants, and produces the baseline needed to know whether levers 1 through 8 move anything. The repeated-panel methodology (Evertune-style, thousands of runs) also fixes the single-run variance problem already noted in project memory.

### Deprioritize

- **llms.txt** (no measured consumption; keep reporting it as informational), **keyword-style content tricks** (failed in GEO and C-SEO Bench both), **backlink campaigns** (weakest correlate in every study), and **chasing stars** (confirmed externally: mentions and copies matter, vanity metrics don't).

**One-sentence synthesis:** training-time prevalence next to the problem makes you the default answer, in-context injection (skills, templates, AGENTS.md, retrieval position) is the only proven way to beat someone else's default, and everything in between is mostly noise with a marketing budget.
